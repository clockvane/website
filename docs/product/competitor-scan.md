## Patterns

### Site structure
- Most sites run: hero -> service list -> logos/testimonials -> process steps -> case studies -> FAQ -> contact form. Large firms (Sapphire) stack 15+ sections, and much of it repeats.
- Smaller, more credible sites (Opstergo, thoughtbot) keep to about 8 sections with a short hero and a repeated single CTA.
- Directory listings (DesignRush) are a major discovery and comparison channel; buyers see budget and rate there before reaching any site.
- Content hubs (blog, guides, tools) are used as trust-building by thoughtbot and Layer3 Labs.

### Messaging
- Common claim set: "tailored", "scalable", "end-to-end", "cutting-edge". Undifferentiated and hard to verify.
- The better ones lead with an outcome or a plain promise (speed, simplicity, reliability; fit your existing operations).
- Industry or problem pages (Layer3 Labs, Devorno) make services feel concrete.

### CTAs
- Primary: free consultation / "Let's talk" / audit / estimate / schedule a meeting. Usually a contact form, sometimes also phone, WhatsApp or email.
- Secondary: case studies, portfolio, resources, newsletter.
- Sapphire offers five different CTAs at once, which dilutes the action. Opstergo and thoughtbot repeat one.

### Proof
- Logos, testimonials, awards and review-site badges dominate. Numeric claims (projects, years, retention) are frequent but self-reported.
- Testimonials with concrete outcomes are rare; Opstergo's have none, Layer3 Labs shows none.

### Pricing display
- Almost nobody publishes prices on their site. Prices are visible mainly through directories or buyer-education articles.
- Layer3 Labs is the exception in spirit: it publishes honest ranges and cost drivers. This is the only visible transparency pattern in the sample.

### Tone
- Spread from hype-heavy (Sapphire) to confident-plain (thoughtbot, Layer3 Labs). Plain tone reads as more trustworthy.

## Differentiation opportunities for Clockvane
1. **Plain, honest copy.** Avoid superlatives and unverifiable stats. Only show proof we can actually back (placeholders until supplied). A new firm cannot match logo walls, so credibility must come from specificity and candour.
2. **Be explicit about the problem: boring manual work.** Lead with named, concrete business chores (the brief's "boring stuff") and what gets automated, rather than a generic service list. This echoes the "runs like clockwork" tagline.
3. **One primary CTA, repeated; a working path with no mailbox.** One conversion (book a call or contact form, form decided later) shown consistently. The contact path must work without a mailbox and be easy to switch (brief constraint). Competitors dilute CTAs or just offer email.
4. **Transparent engagement and pricing stance.** Pricing is rarely shown. Explain how we scope and price, and show cost drivers, with or without numbers, depending on the owner's decision. [[PLACEHOLDER: owner's pricing stance: fixed ranges, "from" prices, or none]].
