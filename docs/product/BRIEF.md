## Change request — 2026-10-29: a clean, futuristic, fast website

*Owner decision. It replaces the visual direction approved at Gate 2 ("Workshop ledger") and the
"calm, not flashy" guidance. Everything else in this brief and the Gate 1 answers still applies.*

### Why
Our website is our first piece of proof. We sell websites, AI automation, AI cost optimization,
cloud and DevOps — a visitor judges in a few seconds whether we can build something excellent, and the
site itself is the evidence. The current pages look like a quiet blog: cream paper, underlined links,
an empty home page. That undersells a technology company. The new site must make every kind of visitor
think *"these people build the future, and they would build mine well"* — while staying clean and fast.

### Who it must impress — all of our customers
| Visitor | What they check in the first 10 seconds | What the site must show them |
|---------|------------------------------------------|------------------------------|
| Small-business owner (automation, website) | Will this save me time? Can I trust them? Is it easy to start? | Clear outcomes, a short path to the free audit call, and a site so polished it answers "can they build mine?" |
| Startup founder or CTO (cloud & DevOps, custom software, AI) | Technical depth, modern engineering, speed | Precision in every detail, technical depth one click away, and a site that loads instantly |
| Operations or IT manager (AI automation, AI cost optimization, cloud) | Risk, security, return on spend | How the work runs, how data stays safe, how costs come down — shown visually, never with invented numbers |
| Anyone comparing agencies | "Is this a template?" | Something they have not seen on other agency sites |

Services to cover (one visual language for all of them): business process automation, AI automation,
**AI cost optimization** (new), cloud & DevOps, websites & apps, custom software
[[PLACEHOLDER: owner to confirm the full service list and what "AI cost optimization" includes]].

### The feel: clean · futuristic · not bloated
1. **Clean first.** Generous space, a strict grid, one idea per section, few elements per screen.
   Remove anything that doesn't help a visitor decide.
2. **Futuristic, with tasteful science-fiction flair.** Precision, depth and light: a dark-first canvas
   with luminous accents, fine grid lines, system-like details (monospace labels, live status chips,
   subtle glow). Sci-fi touches are welcome — holographic or interface-style details, light trails,
   particle or field effects, a cinematic hero moment — when they mean something and fit the limits
   below. Still no clichés: no stock robots or glowing brains, no generic 3D blobs, no purple "AI"
   gradients, no matrix rain, no neon overload.
3. **Motion with meaning.** Every animation explains something real: data flowing through an
   automation, a document turning into structured fields, a pipeline stage turning green, a cost curve
   bending down. Triggered by scroll or by the visitor — never a distracting loop.
4. **One signature moment.** The home page opens with an interactive visual that tells our story — for
   example a living "clockwork" of connected tools where work flows by itself (the Clockvane idea:
   a clock that runs by itself, a vane that shows the direction). The headline is readable instantly;
   the visual enhances it a moment later.
5. **Speed is part of the pitch.** The site must feel instant on a mid-range phone. Showing the visitor
   their own real page-load time (measured in their browser, never invented) is a welcome detail.
6. **Words stay plain.** Futuristic design, plain language (Q12 still applies: no buzzwords).
7. **Calm on request.** With "reduce motion" switched on, the site is static and still beautiful.
   WCAG 2.2 AA everywhere, including contrast on dark surfaces and full keyboard use.

### Experiences to explore (the team decides the details)
- The signature hero (above).
- Services as small living systems: each service shows its own short animation in the same visual
  language, so a visitor *sees* what we do (automation, AI reading a document, cost going down,
  a release pipeline, a website being assembled, custom software).
- "How we work" as a scroll-driven journey.
- Honest proof (Q5): performance of this very site, the stack we use, and the fact that this site is
  built by our own AI engineering team through a real review process (public repository).
- Polished micro-interactions: buttons, links, form fields, smooth page transitions.

### "Not bloated" — hard limits (Lighthouse and CI enforce them)
| Limit | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Lighthouse, mobile, every key page | ≥ 95 in all four categories (unchanged) |
| Largest Contentful Paint / layout shift / interaction | ≤ 2.0 s on mobile lab test / ≤ 0.05 / ≤ 200 ms |
| JavaScript per page (compressed), all effects included | ≤ 35 kB; the hero visual's code ≤ 15 kB |
| CSS per page (compressed) | ≤ 40 kB |
| Fonts | ≤ 3 files, ≤ 120 kB, self-hosted |
| Whole page (compressed) | ≤ 600 kB |
| Not allowed | client-side frameworks for effects, heavy 3D libraries, autoplay video backgrounds, third-party scripts, stock imagery |
| Effects | animate only transform/opacity (smooth on a mid-range Android), pause off-screen and in background tabs, respect reduce-motion and data-saver, fall back to a static design when WebGL is unavailable, and keep all content usable without JavaScript |

### How the team should approach it
1. **Explore before designing.** Study at least 12 excellent current websites of your own choosing —
   AI, developer-tool, cloud and leading design-agency sites — and write down what each does well and
   what we must avoid. Learn, never copy.
2. **Three distinct concepts**, each with a live, working prototype of the home hero and one service
   section, measured against the limits above (Lighthouse report attached) and shown with reduce-motion
   on. Make them span the range — **one restrained and precise, one balanced, one bold and cinematic**
   (the most sci-fi), all within the same limits — so I can see how far to go. I choose one at
   **Gate 2b** (a `gate:2` pull request I merge).
3. **Architecture first for motion**: the tech lead records which techniques are allowed within these
   limits (ADR-004) and updates the performance budgets in `docs/architecture/nfr.md`.
4. Only after Gate 2b: the design system v2, then the shell, home, services and the other pages; the
   pages built so far are restyled with the new system.

### Out of scope
No CMS or blog yet, no paid tools or services, no invented testimonials, logos or statistics (rule 9).

## Change request 2 — 2026-10-30: immersive and unmistakably futuristic

*Owner decision. It replaces the parts of the 2026-10-29 change request named below. Everything else
still applies: who the site must impress, plain words, honest proof, accessibility, no invented facts.*

### Why
I looked at the concept C pages (PRs #93 and #104). They are clean, but **too basic**: boxes joined by
lines, flowcharts and card rows. Nothing there would make anyone stop scrolling. That is not the team's
fault. Our own 29 October limits (35 kB of script, a 15 kB hero, no 3D library) made anything else
impossible. I am lifting those limits. I want a site people remember and share, at the level of the
world's best immersive websites. Research with live references:
[`docs/design/research/immersive-references.md`](../design/research/immersive-references.md).

### What I want
1. **A real-time 3D world, not pictures of diagrams.** The home page is one continuous scene the
   visitor moves through as they scroll. It has light, depth, materials and particles (WebGL, with
   WebGPU where it helps). Scrolling moves the camera through the story.
2. **A signature that is ours.** Build it on the Clockvane idea: precision clockwork that runs by
   itself, and a vane that shows the direction. Make it monumental and alive, so a visitor who moves
   the cursor feels it respond. The team proposes how.
3. **Every service is a transformation in the same world, never a box diagram.** One system changes
   form for each service:
   - business process automation;
   - AI automation;
   - AI cost optimization (chaos becoming an efficient stream);
   - cloud and DevOps;
   - websites and apps;
   - custom software.
4. **Interface details that belong to the world:**
   - shader-based text effects;
   - magnetic, cursor-reactive calls to action;
   - cinematic transitions between sections and pages;
   - optional sound design (off by default, with a visible toggle).
5. **Bold art direction.** Near-black, light as the main material, and one electric accent. Large,
   confident kinetic type. No template layout.
6. **Still readable at once.** The headline and the call to action appear immediately on every device,
   before the 3D finishes loading.
7. **The "wow" test.** Each concept must credibly be an Awwwards Site of the Day nominee. Judge it by:
   would a visitor take a screenshot, or send the link to a friend?

**Not allowed:**
- boxes and arrows, flowcharts, card grids or icon rows as the main visual;
- flat SVG illustration as the hero;
- stock 3D blobs, robots, glowing brains, purple "AI" gradients, matrix rain;
- video backgrounds standing in for real-time graphics.

### New performance limits
These replace the 29 October limits table. Fast still matters, and the discipline now lives in how
we load the 3D, not in banning it.

| Limit | Value |
|-------|-------|
| First paint | Headline, text and call to action are plain HTML/CSS. LCP ≤ 2.5 s on the mobile lab test; layout shift ≤ 0.05 |
| Script before the 3D starts | ≤ 50 kB compressed |
| 3D layer (engine, shaders, scenes) | Loads after first paint, only on capable devices: ≤ 400 kB compressed script, plus ≤ 2 MB of compressed assets on desktop and ≤ 800 kB on phones. three.js is allowed; the tech lead confirms the engine in ADR-005 |
| Lighthouse, mobile | Home and service pages: performance ≥ 90, other categories ≥ 95. All other pages: ≥ 95 in all four |
| Frame rate | 60 fps on a mid-range laptop, ≥ 30 fps on a mid-range Android. GPU tier detection scales particles, resolution and effects |
| Fallbacks | Reduced motion, data saver, no WebGL and low-end GPUs get a beautiful static render of the same world, with all content usable without JavaScript |
| Always | Pause when the tab is hidden or the scene is off-screen; keyboard access and WCAG 2.2 AA throughout |

### How the team should approach it
1. **Designer (Gate 2c):** study the references, then build **two different immersive worlds** as
   **live WebGL prototypes**:
   - each one covers the home hero and the scroll journey through the six services, desktop and phone;
   - put them under `public/labs/<concept>/` with `noindex`, so I can open them on the PR preview
     link from my phone;
   - recommend one; merging the `gate:2` PR approves that recommendation.

   The two concepts must be truly different worlds, not two colourings of one idea.
2. **Tech lead (ADR-005, at the same time):**
   - choose the 3D engine and the loading strategy;
   - set the device tiers and the fallback;
   - set the testing approach (visual regression, frame-rate and size checks in CI);
   - update `docs/architecture/nfr.md` to the limits above.

   ADR-005 replaces the parts of ADR-004 that forbid 3D libraries and set the 35 kB / 15 kB budgets.
3. **After Gate 2c:** the engineers build the immersive layer as a foundation, then the home and services
   pages in it, then the other pages in the same language. The work already merged (design tokens, fonts,
   motion controller, copy deck, contact form) stays where it fits.

### Who works on the design
The designer and the engineers run on the strongest models we have: GPT-6 Astra (high), with Claude
Opus (high) when ChatGPT is at its limit. So do the people who approve the design: the product owner
and the tech lead.
